  3. How does silo venting work during pneumatic filling?

Filling a silo pneumatically.
Where does the air go?

A bulk tanker delivers two streams to the silo: powder and conveying air. The powder stays behind. The air has to find a suitable way out again, even when the ratio between powder and air changes towards the end of the delivery.

To the comparisonWhere do you start?

Short answer

Calculate with the air flow
The tonnes per hour on the delivery note describe the product flow. Also ask about the conveying air during the different phases of the delivery.
Keep two pressures apart
The pressure difference across the filter shows how much resistance air encounters there. The pressure measurement in the silo monitors what the silo wall actually experiences relative to ambient.

Follow the air to outside the silo

During pneumatic filling, conveying air enters with the powder through the fill line. At the same time, the product takes up space and displaces air from the silo. The vent filter lets the outgoing air pass and retains entrained dust. With a pulse-cleaned filter, the separated dust is released again and falls back into the silo.

If the air cannot escape sufficiently, the silo pressure rises. This can happen due to an excessive supply or extra resistance in the vent path. The filter provides the normal air outlet. Pressure monitoring, level monitoring and the prescribed pressure protection each have their own function.

  • Data for the assessment

Also look at the last few minutes

On the delivery note, a load is neatly spread over one discharge time. In practice, the ratio between air and powder changes during that time. Towards the end, the tanker contains less product. If remaining compressed air from the tanker flows uncontrolled into the silo, the air supply can exceed the venting capacity.

In the enquiry, record how the delivery ends and which filling procedure the site and the haulier use. In the event of a pressure alarm, the agreed stop procedure applies. Have the cause assessed before filling is resumed. Safe handling of residual pressure in the tanker is part of the haulier's unloading procedure.

What does the supplier need for filter selection?

State the expected air flow at the conditions at the silo. Give the unit, temperature and reference pressure. A volume flow at standard conditions and an actual volume flow at the silo can give different figures for the same amount of air.

A fine or cohesive powder can behave differently on the medium than a dry, coarser product. So include the product specification, the existing filter type and the cleaning method. Have the filter choice and pressure drop checked against the available vent path and the permissible silo pressure. For combustible dust, explosion safety also requires a separate assessment.

Which value tells you what?

For each trend, ask where the sensor is located and when the measurement was taken. This makes a difference especially for a short deviation at the end of the delivery.

From insight to your situation

Recognise your situation and see which information helps determine the next step.

The alarm always comes towards the end

Investigate the end of the delivery. Together with the haulier, compare the time of the alarm with the transition from product conveying to the final phase. Also have the venting capacity checked.

The pressure difference rises further with each delivery

Compare the filter at the same load. Have the measuring points, cleaning and condition of the medium checked according to the maintenance procedure.

A different bulk tanker will fill the same silo

Request the new delivery data. Compare air flow and unloading procedure with the design basis of the installation before you have this combination approved.

What do you need for initial advice?

  • SiloSilo type, permissible pressure, fill volume and existing monitoring and protection devices.
  • DeliveryProduct, quantity, air flow with reference conditions and course of the discharge cycle.
  • FilterType plate, medium, cleaning and available trends of filter pressure difference and silo pressure.

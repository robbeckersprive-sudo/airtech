Food processing.
Handled hygienically.

Ingredients, extrudates and powders call for an installation that can be cleaned and leaves the product intact. Hygiene, product changeovers and dust explosion risk together determine the design.

What matters
in your industry?

Food processing: the issues we encounter most often here, and what they mean for the design of the installation.

  1. 01

    Cleaning and dismantling

    At product changeovers the installation must be clean quickly and verifiably, dry or wet.

    For the design Avoid dead spaces, make parts accessible and allow tool-free dismantling where possible.

  2. 02

    Materials in contact with the product

    Materials that come into contact with food fall under Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004.

    For the design We specify the choice of materials and seals for each application, with the documentation you need.

  3. 03

    Allergens and cross-contamination

    Residues from a previous batch must not end up in the next one.

    For the design We design product routes, diverters and residual emptying around your cleaning procedure.

  4. 04

    Breakage and dust explosion risk

    Extrudates break at high speed; fine flour, sugar and starch can cause a dust explosion.

    For the design You choose the conveying regime for product integrity; dust data and zone classification drive the design.

How do you prevent breakage of extrudates during conveying?

Breakage is mainly caused by high velocity and hard impacts in bends and at the receiver. Dense phase conveying at a lower velocity is often gentler on the product than dilute phase. The choice depends on the product, the distance and the capacity.

How do you handle product changeovers and allergens?

We start with your cleaning procedure: dry or wet, how often, and what needs to be visible and accessible. We align disassembly, routes and complete emptying with that. Validation of the cleaning remains part of your own food safety system.

What requirements must materials in contact with the product meet?

Materials that come into contact with food fall under Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 and the specific rules for each type of material. When requesting a proposal, ask for the material specification and the documents you need for your quality system.

Is flour or sugar dust explosive?

Many organic powders, such as flour, sugar and starch, can explode in a dust cloud. How sensitive a product is depends on factors including particle size and moisture. Dust data and your zone classification determine the design of filters, valves and pipework.

Can an existing line be extended?

Often, yes. We assess the capacity of the existing blower, the pipe route and the receiving end. Share a drawing or photos and describe what is changing in product or capacity.

Check screener or
grading screen?

At the end of a filling route you want to keep coarse lumps out of the product. After a granulator you want to collect three streams separately. In both cases you use a screen, but the difference is clearest in what has to happen at the outlets.

To the comparisonWhere do you start?

Short answer

Check screening
Choose a check screener if one main stream must stay within specification and you want to intercept occasional oversize.
Grading
Choose a grading screen if you want to separate product streams by particle size. Record per outlet which particles may end up there.

Start with the outlets

Draw the destination of each stream. With check screening, for example, the throughs go to the filler and coarse oversize is kept apart for assessment. With grading, the coarse fraction can go back to a mill, the middle fraction to packing and the fine material to reprocessing.

A single deck already makes two fractions. For a middle fraction with both a lower and an upper limit, two separation steps are needed. They can sit on two decks in one machine. Each deck needs sufficient area and each outlet its own destination.

  • Checking the main stream
  • Dividing particle sizes

What should a check screener stop?

Determine which oversize can occur in the product. Take, for example, a hard lump that could block a filling spout. Describe the shape and size of the lump and of the good product. A long, narrow particle can fall through an opening depending on its orientation.

A lot of good product in the rejects is a signal to look at the feed. If a full bag is tipped onto the deck at once, a thick layer can form. Repeat the trial with a more even feed and weigh the rejects again for the same total input.

How do you test a grading screen?

Suppose you want to pack granules of 1 to 3 mm. In this trial a coarser top deck retains the oversize. The bottom deck lets the fine material through and discharges the middle fraction separately. The limits mentioned are an example of a product specification.

Weigh how much material ends up at each outlet and take a sample from it for a sieve analysis. This shows how much saleable granulate disappears into the return stream and whether the packed fraction meets the specified particle size distribution.

Compare the acceptance criteria

Before the screening trial, record which outcomes the installation must meet. That way you compare two versions on the same assignment.

Choosing a check screener or a grading screen?
Selection questionCheck screenerGrading screen
Which stream counts?Check screenerThe throughs that go to the next process step.Grading screenThe yield and quality per required fraction.
Which deviation do you measure?Check screenerOversize in the throughs and good product in the rejects.Grading screenParticles that are too coarse or too fine in each individual sample.
When do you test capacity?Check screenerAt the highest feed the filling route must handle.Grading screenAt the heaviest load on each deck, also when the recipe changes.
What do you record?Check screenerMesh size, condition of the mesh and mass of the rejects.Grading screenMesh size per deck, mass and particle size distribution per outlet.

From insight to your situation

Recognise your situation and see which information helps determine the next step.

The screen loses capacity after a quarter of an hour

Look at what stays behind in the openings. Stop according to the operating procedure and have the deck inspected. Note whether sticky product adheres to the mesh or particles get stuck in the openings. Include that observation in a follow-up trial.

The middle fraction is clean, the yield low

Investigate the return streams. Re-screen a sample of both the coarse and the fine return stream. The difference shows on which deck the required fraction is lost.

A new recipe has to run on the same machine

Test the new material separately. Keep a sample with the moisture content and particle size distribution that occur during production. Compare outlet quality and capacity with those of the current recipe.

What do you need for initial advice?

  • SpecificationRequired sizes and permissible deviation per outlet.
  • FeedNormal and maximum kg per hour, batch size and discharge duration.
  • Test materialRepresentative product, including the usual lumps, fine fraction and variation in moisture.
